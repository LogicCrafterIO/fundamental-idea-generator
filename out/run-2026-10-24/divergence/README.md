10 of 10 note(s) cite a source for every reason, printed under the reason it supports. 0 reason(s) across 0 note(s) carry none. Every source is an id resolved against that note's `.packet.json`, so the links are the evidence's own rather than the writer's -- a citation the packet cannot vouch for is dropped instead of printed.

### How these were selected

Selected by `fig screen`: 10 note(s) under a **$150** share price and **no market-cap floor**. The share-price cap is a tradeability rule, so a position fits the ~$4,500 capital unit, and the floor keeps the option requirement satisfiable. Loss-making companies and names with no usable option chain are **not** filtered: those facts come from free endpoints, where a bad response is indistinguishable from a genuine absence, and structuring is optional downstream anyway. They are recorded as caveats on the note instead (`--strict` restores the old filters).

Regenerated by any `fig generate` batch, or rebuilt from the notes on disk with `fig index`. Every note is re-linted as it is indexed, so the scores here reflect the current linter rather than the state at generation time.

### Ranking signal

The names here were selected by **revision versus price** (`--variants divergence`); the order below is conviction, as always. A name scores high here when the consensus revision and the 30-day price move point opposite ways -- improving estimates into a falling tape, or deteriorating estimates into a rising one -- and collapses to at most 12 when the two agree. That selects for a name the crowd has taken the other side of, at the cost that the price leg supplies much of the order.
The same screen ran under [`revisions/`](../revisions/) beside this folder -- the same screened rows, one different term -- so the two selections can be compared on identical evidence.

## Ranking

Ordered by conviction, within each side. **This is not a prediction and not a recommendation.** It says where to start reading, using facts about each note rather than a view on the company: catalyst proximity (0.20), estimate-revision agreement across the 7- and 30-day windows (0.20), current lint score (0.10), whether every figure is grounded (0.10), and the sampled end-market verdict (0.40). A high lint score is NOT evidence of a good thesis -- it measures form, and the two highest-scoring notes in this repo's history were the two judged weakest on reasoning. See `src/fig/ranking.py`.
